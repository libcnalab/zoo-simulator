## Models

Every species has a model in `assets/animals/<id>.glb` with a description beside it
(`<id>.json`: clips, their lengths, ground speeds and step events). They are generated by
`tools/animals` (`zoogen`, Blender 4.3 run headless) from one species file each in
`tools/animals/species/`, so a model is rebuilt, not edited:

```bash
OMP_NUM_THREADS=1 blender -b -t 1 --factory-startup --python tools/animals/build_animal.py -- \
    --species lion --out assets/animals --preview build-probe/animal-previews
python3 -m unittest discover -s tools/animals/tests
```

Both the one-thread OpenMP setting and Blender's one-worker `-t 1` are required for reproducible
tangents. Parallel sculpt exports can otherwise differ in the last rounded tangent component
while geometry, textures and animations remain identical.

| Generator | Species |
|---|---|
| `zoogen/sculpt.py`, `bigcat.py` on a prepared third-party sculpt (below) | lion, lion_female, lion_juvenile, tiger, tiger_female |
| `zoogen/rigged.py`, `pachyderm.py`, `pachyderm_motion.py` on a prepared third-party rigged model (below) | rhino, rhino_female, rhino_juvenile, elephant, elephant_female, elephant_juvenile |
| `zoogen/scan.py` on a prepared third-party static sculpt, rigged by us (below) | zebra, zebra_female, zebra_juvenile |
| `zoogen/giraffe2.py` on a prepared head-and-neck scan, body modelled by us (below) | giraffe, giraffe_female, giraffe_juvenile |
| `zoogen/sculpt.py`, `bear.py` on a prepared third-party sculpt, rigged by us (below) | brown_bear, brown_bear_female, brown_bear_juvenile, polar_bear, polar_bear_female, polar_bear_juvenile |
| `zoogen/quadruped.py`, `wild_quadruped.py`, `anatomy.py` | wolf, wolf_female, wolf_juvenile, camel, camel_female, camel_juvenile, meerkat, meerkat_juvenile, lynx, lynx_juvenile, red_panda, red_panda_juvenile |
| `zoogen/birdscan.py` on a prepared third-party scan or sculpt, rigged by us (below) | penguin, penguin_juvenile, flamingo, flamingo_juvenile |
| `zoogen/biped.py` | ostrich, ostrich_female, ostrich_juvenile, kangaroo, kangaroo_female, kangaroo_juvenile |

Each has the same eight clips, which the game picks from what the animal is doing: `idle`,
`walk`, `trot` (the fast gait, a hop for the kangaroo), `eat`, `drink`, `rest`, `sleep`,
`vocalize`; some have activity clips beside them (`sentry`, `dig`, `preen`, `dust_bathe`,
`ruminate`, `filter_feed`, `bathe`, `huddle`, `browse`, below). 8,500 to 41,000 triangles, 18 to 46 bones, a coat painted
by the species file. The authored procedural models in the last two rows are stylised, not
photorealistic, even after their species-specific anatomy passes; what each is weak at is listed in
[`PIVOT_TO_ZOO_EXPLORATION.md`](PIVOT_TO_ZOO_EXPLORATION.md).

### Red panda mother and cubs (2026-11-21)

Red Panda Canopy holds a five-year-old mother and two 0.2-year-old cubs. The
[Smithsonian National Zoo](https://nationalzoo.si.edu/animals/red-panda) supplies the adult mass,
body and tail ranges, bamboo-heavy diet, solitary adult life and mother-with-cubs grouping. The
[EAZA Red Panda Best Practice Guidelines](https://media.eaza.net/uploads/EAZA_Best_Practice_Guidelines_Red_Panda_final_Dec_2021_1_ffc7f12345.pdf)
support the cool planted habitat, shade, elevated resting and climbing choices. Gameplay speed,
ration and price remain explicitly estimated in the species source.

The dedicated adult and cub share a compact low trunk, short dark limbs, broad branch paws,
rounded masked face, pointed ears, cream cheek fans and a long seven-ring tail. The cub has a
shorter 0.24 m body, proportionally broader head, larger eyes, ears and paws, and shorter-stride
locomotion. Both have 33 bones, eight clips and a 1,024 px coat with tangent-space fur relief.
The adult is 19,200 triangles and 2,662,672 bytes; the cub is 17,200 triangles and 2,439,552
bytes. Their single-threaded clean exports match the production GLB and JSON files byte for byte.
The close turntables and the inspected in-game view after 3,000 simulation ticks are in
`evidence/red-panda-canopy-j80-2026-11-21.jpg`. All 155 animation-pipeline tests pass; the final
3,000-tick ASan/UBSan game visit imported both models without a sanitizer finding (leak detection
is disabled because LeakSanitizer cannot run under the debugger/ptrace environment).

### Adult grey-wolf female (2026-11-21)

`wolf_female` gives Wolf Wood's adult female and her 1.3-year-old daughter their own body instead
of the adult male model. The US National Park Service's Yellowstone account gives an average

the species rather than a sex-specific colour.

The 18,528-triangle model has 33 manifest bones and the standard eight clips. Its 2.82 MB
production GLB matches an independent single-threaded export byte for byte. The procedural tests
check that the female is smaller without acquiring juvenile proportions, as well as anatomy,
gait, clip contacts, texture maps and manifest. All 31 game-side tests pass, and a 3,000-tick
AddressSanitizer Wolf Wood run imported the male, female and pup models and kept all six residents
outside. The close `vocalize` viewer and habitat frame are in
`evidence/wolf-female-j68-2026-11-21.jpg`. The slimmer silhouette is visible, while the lofted
joints remain a limitation of the procedural anatomy.

### Grey-wolf anatomy, third pass (2026-11-21)

All three wolf variants now share a canid-specific outline pass. The former head sections moved
backwards as well as forwards along the skull, which made a rounded knot rather than a muzzle;
six strictly advancing sections now form a broad, tapered and blockier snout. A dedicated neck,
narrow deep chest, visible abdominal tuck, leaner digitigrade leg sleeves, large feet, rounded ear
tips and a tail brush which narrows to its tip replace the generic quadruped masses. The older pup
keeps its independently shorter face and proportionally broader cranium, while the adult female
keeps her 95 %-height lighter build.

These choices follow the National Park Service descriptions of wolves as long-legged,
large-footed and narrow-chested relative to domestic dogs
([North Cascades](https://www.nps.gov/noca/learn/nature/wolf-description-and-biology.htm)), with a
thicker, blockier muzzle and smaller, less pointed ears than coyotes
([Denali](https://www.nps.gov/dena/learn/nature/research-mesocarnivores.htm)); the Animal Diversity
Web independently describes the broader snout and shorter ears
([ADW](https://animaldiversity.org/accounts/Canis_lupus/)). This is still authored procedural
anatomy, not a scan or an individual anatomical study.

The adult male and female remain 18,528 triangles and the pup 15,528; all three have 33 manifest
bones and the standard eight clips. Their production GLBs are respectively 2,833,016, 2,920,944
and 2,725,660 bytes and match independent single-threaded exports byte for byte. The before/after
walk, male and female profiles, pup and a 3,000-tick Wolf Wood integration frame are inspected in
`evidence/wolf-anatomy-j74-2026-11-21.jpg`. All 146 animal-pipeline tests and all 305 active CTest
cases pass (21 measurement diagnostics remain disabled); a separate 3,000-tick AddressSanitizer
Wolf Wood run also completed cleanly with leak detection off and all six wolves outdoors.

### What the game does with a model

- **Females and young.** Beside `<id>.glb` the game looks for `<id>_female.glb` and
  `<id>_juvenile.glb` (each with its `.json`). An adult female is drawn with the female model, an
  animal younger than half its species' age of maturity with the juvenile one; a species without
  them uses the one model for everybody. A model is scaled by height:
  `species shoulder height x share of its sex x growth / meta.shoulder_height_m` of the model
  drawn, so a variant states its own height. (Lion, tiger, elephant, zebra, rhinoceros, giraffe,
  camel, wolf, meerkat, ostrich, kangaroo, both bears and penguin have variants so far.)
  Since J24 each alternate is imported only when a current animal needs it; a later birth or an
  animal growing into its adult female body loads that model on the following renderer update.
  Greenhill's adult-only brown- and polar-bear pairs therefore no longer retain their two unused
  cub models. The ordinary visit imports 31 animal models rather than 33 without changing a body
  that is actually present.
- **Swimming.** Where the water is deeper than about 0.6 of the animal's standing height it
  floats: the body rides with `meta.swim_depth_m` (default 0.72 of the model's shoulder height)
  under the surface instead of walking on the pool bed under water, and plays the optional
  clip `swim`. A model may also have `float` for lying still on the water, with its own depth
  (`meta.swim.depth_m` and `meta.float.depth_m`; the penguins have both): under way the animal
  swims, at rest it floats, and the body moves between the two depths smoothly. Rhinoceros,
  elephant, both bears, tiger, penguin and flamingo have `swim`. Every species currently marked
  `needsDeepWater`, including every available female or juvenile model, now has the clip. Species
  with only shallow water still use their land gait there. Seen at Penguin Cove:
  `evidence/penguin-floating-2026-11-13.jpg` (a resting penguin on the pool: the head above
  the water, the body under it; a real one floats a little higher).
- **Water displaced by animals.** Any animal moving through more than 4 cm of water now leaves
  three soft concentric rings at the drawn water surface. Their footprint scales with the body,
  stretches with speed along the heading into a swimming wake, and fades when the animal stops
  or reaches the bank. A floating animal continues to make a quiet disturbance even at rest.
  This applies both to deep swimmers and to flamingos wading on the 0.3 m lagoon shelf; dry
  animals add nothing. The effect reuses one 64 x 64 alpha
  texture and one quad uploaded once by `AnimalRenderer`, then submits one extra draw only for
  each visible wet animal. It is a restrained surface displacement, not a claim of particle
  splashes. Inspected close views: `evidence/animal-water-j59-2026-11-20.jpg`.
- **Activity clips.** A still animal plays the clip its model's `meta.activity_clips` names
  for its activity (`sentinel` -> `sentry`, `preen` -> `preen`, ...; see above), provided the
  model has that clip; it changes into and out of `sentry`, `dust_bathe` and `bathe` with the slower
  0.9 s blend of lying down; standing `huddle` uses the regular blend.
- **Textures.** CNA imports glTF images with one level (its warning GLTF-206), so the game
  builds the mip chain itself when it loads a model (`src/game/AnimalTextures.cpp`). The renderer
  also takes a normal map and an occlusion-roughness-metallic map when a model has them and its
  mesh has tangents; the generator writes neither yet.

### Looking at one animal

```bash
cd build
./zoo-simulator --viewer --animal elephant --clip walk
# a still for comparing with a photograph: seconds into the clip, the side seen, the distance
SDL_VIDEODRIVER=offscreen ./zoo-simulator --viewer --animal elephant --clip walk --at 0.4 \
    --angle 90 --distance 6 --eye 1.6 --frames 20 --screenshot elephant-walk.png
```

`--angle` is in degrees around the animal (0 in front of it, 90 its left side), `--eye` the
height of the camera in metres (a visitor's eyes by default), `--distance` in metres (the whole
animal by default).

State of the six animals named first for realism, as the game draws them (2026-11-13,
`docs/evidence/animals-2026-11-13-before.jpg`): lion and giraffe are recognisable but smooth and
toy-like; the elephant is a flat dark grey with no skin relief; the rhino is nearly black, where
a white rhino is a light dusty grey; the zebra is broken (the head hangs below the chest, stripes
a hand wide); the penguin is acceptable from standing height.
