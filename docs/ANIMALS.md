## Species data

Each species is defined by one JSON file in `data/species/`. Biological values use real-world units; prices and appeal are game values. Minimum enclosure area is scaled to roughly half of common husbandry recommendations for the smallest proper group, and additional-animal space is scaled in proportion. Prices and appeal share one game-wide scale, from inexpensive small species to large, rare, or especially popular animals.

## Models

Every species has a model in `assets/animals/<id>.glb` with a description beside it
(`<id>.json`: clips, their lengths, ground speeds and step events). They are generated by
`tools/animals` (`zoogen`, Blender 4.3 run headless) from one species file each in
`tools/animals/species/`, so a model is rebuilt, not edited:

```bash
blender -b --factory-startup --python tools/animals/build_animal.py -- \
    --species lion --out assets/animals --preview build-probe/animal-previews
python3 -m unittest discover -s tools/animals/tests
```

| Generator | Species |
|---|---|
| `zoogen/sculpt.py`, `bigcat.py` on a prepared third-party sculpt (below) | lion, lion_female, lion_juvenile, tiger |
| `zoogen/rigged.py`, `pachyderm.py`, `pachyderm_motion.py` on a prepared third-party rigged model (below) | rhino, rhino_juvenile, elephant, elephant_female, elephant_juvenile |
| `zoogen/scan.py` on a prepared third-party static sculpt, rigged by us (below) | zebra, zebra_female, zebra_juvenile |
| `zoogen/giraffe2.py` on a prepared head-and-neck scan, body modelled by us (below) | giraffe, giraffe_female, giraffe_juvenile |
| `zoogen/sculpt.py`, `bear.py` on a prepared third-party sculpt, rigged by us (below) | brown_bear, brown_bear_juvenile, polar_bear, polar_bear_juvenile |
| `zoogen/quadruped.py`, `wild_quadruped.py`, `anatomy.py` | wolf, wolf_juvenile, camel, camel_juvenile, meerkat, meerkat_juvenile |
| `zoogen/birdscan.py` on a prepared third-party scan or sculpt, rigged by us (below) | penguin, penguin_juvenile, flamingo, flamingo_juvenile |
| `zoogen/biped.py` | ostrich, ostrich_juvenile, kangaroo, kangaroo_juvenile |

Each has the same eight clips, which the game picks from what the animal is doing: `idle`,
`walk`, `trot` (the fast gait, a hop for the kangaroo), `eat`, `drink`, `rest`, `sleep`,
`vocalize`; some have activity clips beside them (`sentry`, `dig`, `preen`, `dust_bathe`,
`ruminate`, `filter_feed`, `bathe`, `huddle`, `browse`, below). 8,500 to 41,000 triangles, 18 to 46 bones, a coat painted
by the species file. The first-generation models (the last two rows) are stylised, not
photorealistic; what each is weak at is listed in
[`PIVOT_TO_ZOO_EXPLORATION.md`](PIVOT_TO_ZOO_EXPLORATION.md).

### Grey wolf pup and double coat (2026-11-20)

`wolf_juvenile` replaces the scaled-adult wolf used for animals below half the species'
age of maturity. It is an older pup with a 0.46 m shoulder, 0.78 m body, a proportionally
broader skull, larger eyes, ears and paws, and a muzzle shortened to 66 % of the adult
generator's proportion. Its lighter coat is separate from the adult's. The model has 15,528
triangles, 33 bones and the standard eight clips; its short-stride walk is 0.72 m/s and its
trot is 1.65 m/s.

The first juvenile pass was opt-in: a fresh adult export remained byte-for-byte identical and
the production pup matched a separate fresh export. Its viewer comparison and a real Wolf Ridge
frame are in `evidence/wolf-juvenile-j1-2026-11-20.jpg`; the latter shows the game selecting the
pup for one-year-old Rosa.

A second pass improves both ages. A fused, irregular neck ruff breaks the cylindrical chest
outline; the upper legs now enter the body through lean transitions instead of duplicated rings
that read as balls; and the thin cord tail is replaced by a roughened, four-stage tapered brush.
The texture builder can now turn an optional UV-space height field into a packed tangent-space
normal map. Wolves use anisotropic 3D noise for short guard-hair relief while the eyes and wet
nose stay smooth. Both GLBs contain the normal texture and vertex tangents; the adult remains
18,528 triangles (3.09 MB) and the pup 15,528 triangles (2.97 MB), so the change adds surface
detail rather than mesh density. Both production files match independent fresh exports.

The inspected before/after viewers and a real seed-1 Wolf Wood run after 3,000 simulation ticks
are in `evidence/wolf-coat-j10-2026-11-20.jpg`. The relief, ruff and brush make the silhouette and
lighting less toy-like, but the underlying lofted anatomy is still stylised rather than a scan;
the deliberately large pup paws and rounded adult haunches remain visible limitations.

### Meerkat pup (2026-11-20)

`meerkat_juvenile` replaces the scaled-adult bodies of the two 0.15-year-old pups at Meerkat
Lookout. The 0.17 m model has a rounder, proportionally 22 % broader skull, a face shortened
to 72 % of the adult proportion, larger eyes, ears and paws, and a lighter coat. Its gait is
shorter and slower (0.42 m/s walk, 1.05 m/s trot). It has 8,576 triangles, 33 bones and ten
clips: the standard eight plus the adult's dedicated `sentry` and `dig` activities, rescaled
so the hind feet and tail still support the upright guard and the paws still meet the ground.

A fresh adult export remained byte-for-byte identical after the variant refactor, and the
production pup matches an independent fresh export. The same-camera size comparison, four
shape views and a real habitat frame are in
`evidence/meerkat-juvenile-j2-2026-11-20.jpg`; the HUD identifies the selected model's animal
as two-month-old Pavel. The shape reads as a pup.

A second pass improves both ages without moving the skeleton or foot targets used by sentry and
dig. The middle torso and limb lofts are 8-15 % leaner, replacing the barrel body and swollen
legs. Every foot now has four separate toes and four non-retractile claws; the front claws are
longer for digging. An anisotropic tangent-space normal map adds short longitudinal guard-hair
relief while keeping eyes, nose and claws smooth. Both variants now have 11,648 triangles, 33
bones and the same ten clips; the adult GLB is 2.46 MB and the pup 2.53 MB. Both production
exports match independent fresh builds. The grounded sentry tripod and alternating dig stroke
were inspected again after the change.

The same-camera comparisons and a seed-1 Meerkat Lookout run after 3,000 simulation ticks are in
`evidence/meerkat-coat-j12-2026-11-20.jpg`. The body and working feet read more like a meerkat,
but the head and underlying lofted anatomy remain stylised and the coat has no individual hair
cards.

### Bactrian camel calf (2026-11-20)

`camel_juvenile` replaces the uniformly scaled adults previously used for the two young animals
at Red Dune. The 1.26 m calf has a slimmer barrel, a shorter face and neck, proportionally larger
head, eyes, ears and split pads, and two low developing humps rather than the adult's tall full
humps. Its separate lighter sand coat keeps the calf readable beside the adults. The short-stride
walk is 0.72 m/s and the fast pace is 1.55 m/s. The model has 20,640 triangles, 36 bones and nine
clips: the standard eight plus the adult's ten-second `ruminate` jaw grind.

The calf's drinking pose targets the visible water surface while keeping its head and ear roots
above ground; all clip joints, foot plants and loop boundaries are checked by the pipeline. A
fresh adult export remained byte-for-byte identical after the opt-in generator change, and the
production calf matches an independent final export. The game loaded the calf variant in a real
Red Dune run and selected nine-month-old Ivo. The same-camera
adult comparison, close calf viewer, four shape views and habitat frame are in
`evidence/camel-juvenile-j4-2026-11-20.jpg`. Its age silhouette is distinct.

A second pass improves both ages. An anisotropic tangent-space normal map gives the flat painted
coat longitudinal guard-hair relief while leaving the pads, eyes and new nails smooth. A fused,
roughened loft widens the crown and sides of the neck into a winter mane, with a small forelock;
the thin tail now ends in a four-stage rough tuft. Each broad split pad has two separate dark
nails, mirrored across all four feet. The additions bring both models to 21,760 triangles and 36
bones without changing their nine clips; the adult GLB is 3.50 MB and the calf 3.52 MB. Both
production exports match independent fresh builds.

The inspected same-camera comparisons and a seed-1 Red Dune run after 3,000 simulation ticks are
in `evidence/camel-coat-j11-2026-11-20.jpg`. The adult's full humps and the calf's low developing
humps remain clearly distinct. The underlying lofted anatomy and face are still simple rather
than scan-based, and the coat has no individual long hair cards.

### Big cats from a sculpt (2026-11-13)

Procedural geometry did not reach a believable cat, so the lion, the lioness, the cub and
the tiger are built on a professional sculpt under a licence that allows it: "Free strong
lioness - 4k" by Pedro B. Goulart (Pebegou), CC BY 4.0 (`assets/ATTRIBUTION.md`). The licence was
read on 2026-11-13 from Sketchfab's public record of the model
(`api.sketchfab.com/v3/models/84d56e6cfcf247cb8fe468312f371677`: "CC Attribution",
`creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0`, downloadable).
`tools/animals/prepare_bigcat.py` turns the download into a small derivative
(`tools/animals/sources/bigcat/`, 4.7 MB since S7: decimated mesh with its skin weights under
our bone names and the source's colour, normal and occlusion maps); the species files re-proportion it per
bone (heavier male, short-legged cub with larger head, ears and paws, longer tiger), paint
the coat anew and add mane, tail tuft and cheek ruff. The original has no animation: the
eight clips are ours, as for every other species.

| Model | Triangles | Shoulder | What it is |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| `lion` | 40,574 | 1.15 m | male with a mane of hair cards over an opaque backing |
| `lion_female` | 32,398 | 1.05 m | the game draws it for every female lion |
| `lion_juvenile` | 23,122 | 0.45 m | drawn below half of the age of maturity |
| `tiger` | 32,390 | 1.00 m | longer body, broad paws, striped coat |

(Triangles after the second pass, S7.)

Seen in Blender previews, in the game's viewer and in the lion habitat
(`evidence/big-cats-sculpt-2026-11-13.jpg`). Honest state: the lioness is the good one.
The male still has the lioness's head inside a dark collar (a real mane starts on the
forehead and the cheeks and the skull is broader); the tiger's face markings and chest are
visibly painted by rule and its body is a lioness's; the cub keeps adult shoulder muscles.
No cub lives in the Visitor Zoo yet, so `lion_juvenile` is only seen in the viewer.

**Second pass (S7, 2026-11-14).** The sculpt's own material is used now: its 1024 px base
colour, tangent-space normal map and packed occlusion map (`sources/bigcat/base.png`,
`normal.png`, `orm.png`; CC BY 4.0 like the mesh) carry the eyes, nose leather, lips and fur
direction that the procedural coat had replaced; `zoogen/bigcat_coat.py` recolours them per
species (tiger stripes irregular and asymmetric, rosettes on the cub). The jaw is closed by
31.5 degrees and head, jaw and ear density are kept through the decimation. The male's mane,
the tail tufts and the tiger's cheek ruff are skinned hair cards (`zoogen/bigcat_hair.py`) in
a second material, `alphaMode: MASK`, cutoff 0.5, double-sided, over an opaque backing that
casts the shadow. The game gives alpha-tested textures mip levels that keep the full-size
coverage (`src/game/AnimalTextures.cpp`, the engine's `MipOptions::alphaTestReference`), so the
mane stays full at 30 m. Seen in Blender and in the game's viewer
(`evidence/big-cats-s7-2026-11-14.jpg`): the male finally has a lion's head inside a mane, the
lioness's face is the sculptor's. Still wrong: the tiger's cheek ruff reads as a grey comb
close up, the faces are not at photograph quality (Codex's own verdict), and the mane's cards
are visible as cards at arm's length. Screenshots of hair taken with `--screenshot` keep the
cards' alpha in the PNG; view them flattened (`docs/UPSTREAM.md`, ZOO-UP-002).

### Rhinoceros and elephant on rigged models (2026-11-13)

The same step for the two pachyderms (Codex task S6): `tools/animals/prepare_rigged.py` turns
a downloaded rigged model into a small derivative (`tools/animals/sources/rhino/` 5.1 MB,

occlusion maps, the source's walk loop resampled), and `zoogen/rigged.py` with
`pachyderm.py` builds the game's models from it. Sources and licence: `assets/ATTRIBUTION.md`,

so face and ears are a conversion and read as one from close by. The calf keeps adult skin.
No calf lives in the Visitor Zoo yet, so both juveniles are only seen in the viewer.

### Elephant, second pass, and giraffe (2026-11-14)

The rest/sleep pass J7 replaces the old rolled standing pose used for elephant sleep. That pose
turned the columnar legs towards the camera, so a sleeping animal looked upside down with four
round soles spread around its belly. The source rig cannot bend those legs into a sound lateral
recumbency without deforming the skin. All three variants now use a supported standing doze,
which is also a real elephant sleep posture: the head drops by more than 1.5 % of shoulder height,
the ears become quiet, the trunk makes a low relaxed curl, three feet remain planted and one hind
foot relaxes 1 % of shoulder height above the ground. The ordinary `rest` remains a more alert
standing posture. The before/after, revised zebra rest and a real 18:00 habitat frame identifying
Nora as asleep are in `evidence/resting-poses-j7-2026-11-20.jpg`. All 115 animal-pipeline tests
and all 267 active CTest cases pass.

### Zebra from a static sculpt (2026-11-13)

The zebra's source has no skeleton at all (Codex task S8): "Zebra Anatomy" by Shikaki_Naito,
CC BY 4.0, an anatomy study of which one half is a cutaway. `tools/animals/prepare_scan.py`
keeps the intact flank, mirrors it, welds the seam, varies the stripes of one side, decimates
(head and hooves kept denser) and writes a 1.9 MB derivative (`tools/animals/sources/zebra/`:
mesh and a 2048x1024 coat; the source has no normal map). `zoogen/scan.py` fits **our**
quadruped skeleton to landmarks measured on the mesh (given in `species/zebra.py`), computes
skin weights (bone heat, then corrected: four influences, limbs kept off the belly) and
builds the three models. This is the path for every further species whose best source is a
static sculpt or scan.

| `zebra_juvenile` | 19,500 | 33 | 0.90 m | foal: long legs, short body, brown stripes |

Nine clips each, ours: a four-beat lateral walk with the head nodding, a diagonal trot with

Seen in the game's viewer and in the savannah habitat (`evidence/zebra-scan-2026-11-13.jpg`:

J7 keeps that grounded sternal silhouette but removes the statue-like hold from `rest`: breathing

`flamingo_juvenile` replaces the scaled pink adults previously used for the 0.8- and 0.9-year-old
birds at Flamingo Lagoon. These are nearly grown first-year juveniles, not downy hatchlings: the
licensed adult sculpt and fitted rig are retained at 86 % height, while a separate coat replaces
the crimson coverts with light grey-brown and the first muted pink feathers, and gives the bill and
legs juvenile grey tones. The Smithsonian National Zoo describes first-birthday flamingos as
light pink and notes that the darker adult colour takes another one or two years. The walk is
0.60 m/s and the trot 1.25 m/s.

The model retains all eleven adult clips, including one-leg rest and sleep, `preen`, `filter_feed`
and `huddle`; every target distance and gait displacement is scaled with the bird. A fresh adult
export remained byte-for-byte identical after the opt-in change, and the production juvenile
matches an independent fresh export. The same-camera comparison, four scan views and a real
Flamingo Lagoon binocular frame are in `evidence/flamingo-juvenile-j6-2026-11-20.jpg`; the HUD
identifies selected Aya as female and ten months old. The age and coat now read correctly, but the
variant deliberately shares the coarse feather ridges, folded-leg limitations and lack of a normal
map of the adult sculpt.

The penguins have ten clips: the usual eight, `swim` (flying under water with the flippers,
0.45 m of the bird under the surface, 1.5 m/s) and `float` (resting on the surface, 0.25 m
under, 0.12 m/s); the manifest says how deep the bird lies in each (`meta.swim`,
`meta.float`). 11 new tests; the two older tests that measured the lofted penguin were
adapted by the lead (the height is measured on the scan mesh, three flipper joints expected).

Seen in the game's viewer and at Penguin Cove (`evidence/penguin-flamingo-scan-2026-11-13.jpg`).
Honest state: both are real birds' shapes now where the old ones were bowling pins; the
penguin is slimmer than the old one and stands like a penguin. What is wrong: belly and feet
keep the roughness and the asymmetry of a mounted specimen; the bill does not really open;
all adults share one spot layout; the flamingo's feather ridges and deep neck bends are
coarse, its tucked head and folded leg do not bear a close look; neither has a normal map;
the first-year flamingo deliberately shares that sculpt rather than pretending to be a downy chick.

A species without a model would be left out of the Visitor Zoo by the game (its habitat built
and standing empty).

The manifest maps an activity to a clip (`meta.activity_clips`, activity names as the save
format writes them); `AnimalRenderer` plays the clip where the model has it and keeps its
default otherwise (`dig` and `filter_feed` play `eat`, `bathe` plays `rest`, the rest `idle`).
The bill and muzzle postures were fitted numerically against the models' own skin: a preening
bill tip lies on the plumage, 0.5 to 3 cm out from the scan's surface, at the held moments
(`tests/test_activity_clips.py`). Every clip that existed before is bit for bit the same pose
(compared bone by bone against the previous pipeline); the meerkat's `idle` remains the
sitting-up watch it always was.

Not made: preening for the ostrich (its routine never preens), group-synchronised huddles beyond
individual poses, and paired social-contact clips.

the flamingo's bill and neck are buried in the wing when it preens there, as a real one's are, so

### What the game does with a model

- **Females and young.** Beside `<id>.glb` the game looks for `<id>_female.glb` and
  `<id>_juvenile.glb` (each with its `.json`). An adult female is drawn with the female model, an
  animal younger than half its species' age of maturity with the juvenile one; a species without
  them uses the one model for everybody. A model is scaled by height:
  `species shoulder height x share of its sex x growth / meta.shoulder_height_m` of the model
  drawn, so a variant states its own height. (Lion, elephant, zebra, rhinoceros, giraffe, wolf,
  meerkat, ostrich, both bears and penguin have variants so far.)
- **Swimming.** Where the water is deeper than about 0.6 of the animal's standing height it
  floats: the body rides with `meta.swim_depth_m` (default 0.72 of the model's shoulder height)
  under the surface instead of walking on the pool bed under water, and plays the optional
  clip `swim`. A model may also have `float` for lying still on the water, with its own depth
  (`meta.swim.depth_m` and `meta.float.depth_m`; the penguins have both): under way the animal
  swims, at rest it floats, and the body moves between the two depths smoothly. Rhinoceros,
  elephant, polar bear and penguin have `swim`; the other species still paddle with their walk
  clip. Seen at Penguin Cove:
  `evidence/penguin-floating-2026-11-13.jpg` (a resting penguin on the pool: the head above
  the water, the body under it; a real one floats a little higher).
- **Activity clips.** A still animal plays the clip its model's `meta.activity_clips` names
  for its activity (`sentinel` -> `sentry`, `preen` -> `preen`, ...; see above), provided the
  model has that clip; it changes into and out of `sentry`, `dust_bathe` and `bathe` with the slower
  0.9 s blend of lying down; standing `huddle` uses the regular blend.
- **Textures.** CNA imports glTF images with one level (its warning GLTF-206), so the game
  builds the mip chain itself when it loads a model (`src/game/AnimalTextures.cpp`). The renderer
  also takes a normal map and an occlusion-roughness-metallic map when a model has them and its
  mesh has tangents; the generator writes neither yet.

### Looking at one animal

```bash
cd build
./zoo-simulator --viewer --animal elephant --clip walk
# a still for comparing with a photograph: seconds into the clip, the side seen, the distance
SDL_VIDEODRIVER=offscreen ./zoo-simulator --viewer --animal elephant --clip walk --at 0.4 \
    --angle 90 --distance 6 --eye 1.6 --frames 20 --screenshot elephant-walk.png
```

`--angle` is in degrees around the animal (0 in front of it, 90 its left side), `--eye` the
height of the camera in metres (a visitor's eyes by default), `--distance` in metres (the whole
animal by default).

State of the six animals named first for realism, as the game draws them (2026-11-13,
`docs/evidence/animals-2026-11-13-before.jpg`): lion and giraffe are recognisable but smooth and
toy-like; the elephant is a flat dark grey with no skin relief; the rhino is nearly black, where
a white rhino is a light dusty grey; the zebra is broken (the head hangs below the chest, stripes
a hand wide); the penguin is acceptable from standing height.
